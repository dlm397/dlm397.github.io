Economics

Econometrics, PhD

Course Description: A rigorous graduate-level treatment of modern econometric methods, with emphasis on identification, inference, causal design, and the statistical properties of empirical models used in applied economics.

Key topics covered
  • Classical linear regression, omitted variable bias, and Gauss-Markov results
  • Inference, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and asymptotic reasoning
  • Instrumental variables, simultaneity, and two-stage least squares
  • Panel data methods, fixed effects, random effects, and clustered standard errors
  • Difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity, and field experiments
  • Heteroskedasticity, robust inference, and bootstrap methods
  • Binary, ordered, and count outcome models
  • Maximum likelihood, GMM, and discrete-choice modeling

Mathematics

Monte Carlo Methods

Course Description: A rigorous introduction to simulation-based inference, numerical approximation, and stochastic modeling, with emphasis on random number generation, sampling schemes, variance reduction, and Markov chain methods.

Key topics covered
  • Pseudorandom number generation and simulation design
  • Sampling from distributions via inversion, rejection, and alias methods
  • Monte Carlo integration and confidence interval construction
  • Variance reduction techniques, including control variates and stratification
  • Markov chains, invariant distributions, and MCMC
  • Poisson processes and introductory Itô-process thinking

Topology

Course Description: A rigorous study of point-set topology, metric spaces, continuity, compactness, connectedness, and the basic structures that underpin modern analysis.

Key topics covered
  • Topological spaces, bases, and continuity
  • Metric spaces, convergence, and completeness
  • Connectedness, compactness, and separation axioms
  • Nets and generalized convergence
  • Normed spaces, inner product spaces, Banach spaces, and Hilbert spaces

Real Analysis

Course Description: A rigorous study of measure and integration, Banach and Hilbert space methods, and classical complex analysis, following the structure and scope of Rudin's Real and Complex Analysis, 3rd ed.

Key topics covered
  • Abstract integration, measurability, and simple functions
  • Measures, positive and complex integration, and sets of measure zero
  • Positive Borel measures, Lebesgue measure, and regularity properties
  • Lp spaces, approximation by continuous functions, and basic functional analysis
  • Hilbert space theory, orthonormal sets, and trigonometric series
  • Banach spaces, Baire category, and the Hahn-Banach theorem
  • Complex measures, total variation, and the Radon-Nikodym theorem
  • Differentiation of measures and the fundamental theorem of calculus
  • Product spaces, product measures, and Fubini's theorem
  • Fourier transforms, inversion, and Plancherel theory
  • Holomorphic functions, Cauchy's theorem, residues, and power series
  • Harmonic functions, Poisson integrals, and the maximum modulus principle
  • Conformal mapping and basic approximation theory

Linear Algebra II

Course Description: An advanced linear algebra course focused on canonical forms, spectral theory, orthogonality, and applications to differential equations and stochastic systems.

Key topics covered
  • Linear transformations, kernels, ranges, and matrix representations
  • Eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and diagonalization
  • Jordan canonical form and minimal polynomials
  • Inner product spaces, orthogonality, and least squares
  • Quadratic forms and spectral decomposition
  • Applications to differential equations and Markov chains
